# Public Knowledge Ledger Constitution

**Draft version:** v0.1

## Purpose

The Public Knowledge Ledger (PKL) exists to record and evaluate knowledge claims through transparent evidence, provenance, independent verification, challenge, correction, and uncertainty.

## Article I — Evidence over authority

No claim is accepted merely because it comes from a particular person, organisation, AI, institution, or government.

## Article II — Provenance

Every substantive claim should identify the evidence and sources on which it rests whenever such information is available.

## Article III — Transparency

The reasoning behind an evidence or claim assessment should be inspectable.

## Article IV — Challenge

Any participant may challenge a claim by presenting relevant evidence or reasoning.

## Article V — Preservation

A challenged, superseded, or disproven claim must not be silently erased. Its history remains available.

## Article VI — Uncertainty

PKL must be capable of explicitly recording unknown, uncertain, disputed, provisional, and strongly supported states.

## Article VII — Independent verification

Independent confirmation carries greater evidential value than repetition of the same underlying source.

## Article VIII — Contributor reputation

Contributors may build reputations through demonstrated accuracy, transparency, and rigorous collaboration. Reputation never substitutes for evidence and never determines truth by itself.

## Article IX — Equal participation

Humans and AI systems may contribute. Neither receives automatic authority over the truth of a claim.

## Article X — Blind evaluation

Where practical, evidence should initially be evaluated without revealing the contributor's identity, reducing authority and status bias.

## Article XI — Correction

When new evidence changes an assessment, PKL records the change and its justification rather than rewriting history.

## Article XII — Auditability

The ledger's history should be cryptographically verifiable where technically practical.

## Article XIII — Open inspection

The rules, methodology, and—where legally and ethically possible—the underlying data should be available for independent inspection.

## Article XIV — No claim of absolute truth

PKL records conclusions supported by available evidence. It does not claim that its classifications constitute metaphysical or absolute truth.

## Article XV — Self-correction

The rules governing PKL are themselves subject to criticism, testing, and transparent revision.

## Article XVI — Independent evidence must be validated

Evidence should not be treated as independent merely because it appears in a different publication or comes from a different contributor. Where possible, PKL should trace provenance and explicitly assess independence.

## Article XVII — Do not compress away important uncertainty

PKL should not reduce complex evidence to a single confidence number when the underlying dimensions materially affect interpretation. Individual evidence dimensions must remain inspectable.

## Foundational principle

> **Don't trust the person. Don't trust the AI. Follow the evidence.**
